Quick Start in 4 Easy Steps

                    

                    

                        

                            Step 1
                            
Set Up Your Robot Base

                            
Go to the Robot tab and enter your wheel diameter and axle width, or pick a ready preset in Tuning (like standard SPIKE Prime).

                        

                        

                            Step 2
                            
Click & Drag Your Path

                            
Click anywhere on the FLL mat canvas to add waypoints, or drag existing waypoints and orange direction handles to orient your robot.

                        

                        

                            Step 3
                            
Test Drive in Simulator

                            
Press Space or click the blue  Play button to preview your robot's exact turn-and-drive sequence in real-time.

                        

                        

                            Step 4
                            
Export & Run Your Code

                            
Click Export to get clean Pybricks Python, SPIKE Prime Python, or Block instructions equipped with built-in Gyro Correction!

                        

                    

                


                
                

                    

                        
                        
How to Use Features

                    


                    

                        

                            

                                 1. Waypoints & Actions on Arrival
                            

                            

                                Waypoints are goal points on the mat. Your robot turns toward a waypoint, drives straight to it, and turns to match the waypoint's heading angle.
                            

                            
    
                                
  • Drag Target: Drag the center dot to move the location; drag the orange handle to rotate the target angle.
    • 
                                
  • Actions on Arrival: Click + Add... on any waypoint card to program actions when the robot reaches that spot—such as pausing, spinning attachment motors, or playing a sound.
    • 
                                
  • Optimize Path: Click the green Optimize path button to automatically delete unnecessary intermediate points along straight lines!
    • 
                            

                        


                        

                            

                                 2. Launch Snapping & Wall Positioning
                            

                            

                                In FLL, placing your robot squarely against the border wall is key for repeatable runs.
                            

                            
    
                                
  • Snap SW / SE: Instantly snaps Waypoint 1 into the official Southwest or Southeast launch arcs.
    • 
                                
  • Wall Dropdown: Select Bottom, Top, Left, or Right to force Waypoint 1 flush against a wall taking your robot's exact chassis dimensions into account.
    • 
                                
  • Gap Offset: Add a small gap (e.g. 5 mm) if your attachment extends past your chassis frame.
    • 
                            

                        


                        

                            

                                 3. BIOGLOW™ Mission Interactive Scorer
                            

                            

                                Track points for the official 2026–27 BIOGLOW™ challenge right inside the tool!
                            

                            
    
                                
  • Missions Tab: Expand mission cards to check off completed objectives and calculate your score automatically.
    • 
                                
  • Canvas Markers: Click numbered mission dots on the map to open the corresponding mission details card instantly.
    • 
                                
  • Move Markers: Check "Move markers" to drag mission points so they match your custom mat alignment.
    • 
                            

                        


                        

                            

                                 4. Exporting Gyro-Corrected Code
                            

                            

                                Path Studio generates code that uses your hub's IMU Gyro sensor to keep the robot moving perfectly straight and turning accurately on the physical mat.
                            

                            
    
                                
  • Select your environment: Pybricks Python, Pybricks Blocks, SPIKE Prime Python, EV3 MicroPython, or Human Driver Steps.
    • 
                                
  • Click Copy Code and paste it straight into your robotics software!
    • 
                                
  • Gyro gains: Drive Kp, Turn Kp, and Turn tolerance from the Tuning tab are written into every export so the physical robot matches what you tuned.
    • 
                            

                        

                    

                


                
                

                    

                        
                        

                            
Step-by-Step Robot Physical Tuning Guide

                            
Easy instructions for middle school students to calibrate their real robot to match the screen!

                        

                    


                    
                    

                        
                        

                            Why does my physical robot not move like the screen?

                            LEGO plastic wheels squish slightly under weight, and wheel axle spacing can differ by a few millimeters. Calibration fixes distance and turning errors so your robot drives on the mat exactly like it does in the simulator!
                        

                    


                    
                    

                        

                            

                                A
                                Calibrating Driving Distance (Wheel Diameter)
                            

                            Fixes over-shooting or stopping short
                        


                        

                            

                                STEP 1
                                
In the Tuning tab, click 50 cm Straight Test Path.

                            

                            

                                STEP 2
                                
Set your robot up against the back wall on your real FLL mat. Place a ruler or tape measure along the edge of your robot.

                            

                            

                                STEP 3
                                
Export and run the code. Measure how far the robot actually moved on the mat with your ruler (e.g. 48.5 cm instead of 50 cm).

                            

                            

                                STEP 4
                                
Under Distance Calibration on the Tuning tab, enter 50 in Commanded Distance and 48.5 in Actual Measured Distance, then click Adjust Wheel Diameter.

                            

                        


                        

                            
                            Result: Your Wheel Diameter parameter automatically updates to eliminate distance errors!
                        

                    


                    
                    

                        

                            

                                B
                                Calibrating Pivot Turns (Track Width / Autotune)
                            

                            Fixes under-turning or over-spinning
                        


                        

                            

                                STEP 1
                                
In the Tuning tab, click 180° Spin Test.

                            

                            

                                STEP 2
                                
Align your robot precisely parallel to a grid line on your mat.

                            

                            

                                STEP 3
                                
Export and run the code. Watch where the robot stops spinning. Did it complete a half turn (facing the opposite way), or did it stop early / turn too far?

                            

                            

                                STEP 4
                                
Under Turn Calibration (Autotune), enter 180 in Target Angle and your actual measured turn angle (e.g. 190°) in Actual Measured Turn Angle, then click Adjust Track Width.

                            

                        


                        

                            
                            Result: Your Axle Track Width parameter automatically re-calculates for perfect turns!
                        

                    


                    
                    

                        

                            

                                C
                                Tuning Gyro Correction Gains (Kp & Tolerance)
                            

                            Written into every export
                        


                        

                            After distance and turn calibration look good, open the Tuning tab and find
                            Gyro Correction Gains (PID / Kp). These numbers control how hard the robot fights heading error while driving and turning. They are injected into Pybricks, SPIKE, EV3, Blocks, and Human exports.
                        


                        

                            

                                
Drive Kp

                                
Default 2.0

                                
How strongly the robot steers back to the target heading while driving straight. Raise if it drifts; lower if it weaves or oscillates.

                            

                            

                                
Turn Kp

                                
Default 1.8

                                
How aggressively the robot pivots toward the target angle. Raise for snappier turns; lower if it overshoots and hunts.

                            

                            

                                
Turn tolerance (°)

                                
Default 1.0°

                                
Stop the turn when the remaining error is this small or less. Use 1–2° for most mats; tighter only if your hub is very stable.

                            

                        


                        

                            

                                STEP 1
                                
Export and run a long straight (e.g. 50 cm). If the robot drifts left/right, raise Drive Kp a little (try +0.5). If it zig-zags, lower Drive Kp.

                            

                            

                                STEP 2
                                
Run the 180° spin test. If the turn ends short or overshoots then corrects, adjust Turn Kp and/or Turn tolerance until it settles cleanly.

                            

                            

                                STEP 3
                                
Click Export again so the new gains appear in the generated code. Gains are also saved with your project (Save / autosave).

                            

                        


                        

                            
                            Tip: Change one number at a time and re-test. Use Reset gains to defaults if you get lost. Typical useful ranges: Drive Kp 1.0–4.0, Turn Kp 1.0–3.0, tolerance 0.5–2.0°.
                        

                    

                


                
                

                    

                        
                        

                            
PID Tuning Guide

                            
What proportional control is, why FLL uses P-only, and how to tune Drive Kp / Turn Kp on the mat

                        

                    


                    

                        
                        

                            PID is a classic feedback control method. You measure an error, then use up to three terms (P, I, D) to decide how hard to correct.
                            In Path Studio the gyro loops are P-only — that is intentional and common for FLL tank-drive heading control.
                        

                    


                    

                        
The formula

                        

                            output = Kp × error + Ki × (sum of past error) + Kd × (rate of change of error)
                        

                        

                            

                                
P — Proportional

                                
“How far am I right now?” Corrects harder when farther from the target. High → oscillates / weaves. Low → slow, may leave a steady offset.

                            

                            

                                
I — Integral

                                
“How long have I been wrong?” Removes leftover steady-state error that P alone can’t fix. High → overshoot and windup. Rarely needed for FLL gyro drive.

                            

                            

                                
D — Derivative

                                
“How fast is the error changing?” Damps motion and reduces overshoot. High → noisy / twitchy (amplifies gyro noise). Often left at zero for FLL.

                            

                        

                    


                    

                        
Why FLL gyro code is usually just P

                        
    
                            
  • The robot is continuously moving; you don’t need perfect zero error every millisecond.
    • 
                            
  • Gyro noise is real — a strong D term often makes the robot jitter.
    • 
                            
  • Integral can “wind up” if the robot is blocked or a turn never quite finishes.
    • 
                        

                        
So a well-tuned P (plus a small turn tolerance) is usually enough. That is exactly what Drive Kp, Turn Kp, and Turn tolerance control in the Tuning tab.

                    


                    

                        
How the gains feel on a real robot

                        
    
                            
  • Drive Kp too low → drifts and doesn’t fight heading error. Too high → left/right weaving.
    • 
                            
  • Turn Kp too low → soft, slow pivots that may stop short. Too high → overshoots and hunts.
    • 
                            
  • Tolerance too tight (e.g. 0.2°) → turn may never finish if the gyro is noisy. Too loose → inaccurate headings for the next segment.
    • 
                        

                    


                    

                        
Step-by-step mat procedure

                        

                            1
                            
Calibrate wheel diameter and track width first (Parts A & B above). Gains cannot fix wrong robot dimensions.

                        

                        

                            2
                            
Start with defaults: Drive Kp 2.0, Turn Kp 1.8, tolerance 1.0°.

                        

                        

                            3
                            
Export a long straight. If the robot drifts, raise Drive Kp by ~0.5. If it weaves, lower Drive Kp. Change only one number, re-export, re-test.

                        

                        

                            4
                            
Run a 90° or 180° spin. Adjust Turn Kp and tolerance until the robot settles cleanly without hunting.

                        

                        

                            5
                            
Re-export so the final gains appear in the generated code. Gains are saved with your project.

                        

                    


                    

                        Classic tuning order: Start with I = 0 and D = 0 (pure P). Raise Kp until the robot responds quickly but still settles (or just starts to oscillate, then back off ~20–30%). Change one gain at a time and re-test the same motion. Typical useful ranges for this studio: Drive Kp 1.0–4.0, Turn Kp 1.0–3.0, tolerance 0.5–2.0°.
